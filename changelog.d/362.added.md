Bicycle codes as a second construction of the parity-check problem class: `sim.ldpc.bicycle_code` builds `H = [A | A^T]` from a sparse circulant and deletes rows to the target rate, as edges rather than as an array, so the 19,998-bit code draws without a 200 MB matrix. The channels, the decoder and the oracles are the Gallager code's, and three fixtures at 12, 96 and 996 bits match the Gallager fixtures in length, degrees and design rate. The construction is refereed exactly -- degrees, `k = n - rank H` across four deletion counts, the self-orthogonality `H H^T = 0` over GF(2), and minimum distance 4 over the 12-bit fixture's 64 enumerated codewords -- and the decoding is compared with a Gallager draw on shared seeds: more blocks failed at all six 96-bit settings and at eleven of twelve 996-bit ones.

The quantum half: `sim.css.CssCode` is the Calderbank–Shor–Steane code `Hx = Hz = H` defines over a bicycle matrix, with `k = n - 2 rank(H)`, a basis of `ker(H) / rowspace(H)`, and the four-cycle count the CSS condition forces into the Tanner graph. A matrix with `H H^T != 0` and a code with `k = 0` are both refused, the second being what the classical fixtures give at `m = n / 2`; two instances are declared at `m < n / 2`, [[16, 2]] with 30 four-cycles and [[96, 16]] with 160. `likelihood.css` scores a decode on whether the residual is a stabilizer rather than on whether it is the error, and carries exact degenerate maximum likelihood -- the coset of greatest summed probability -- by enumerating every error where `2 ** n` reaches. At the 16-qubit instance that floor is a logical error rate of 0.070297, against 0.072735 for the decoder maximizing a single error's probability; sum-product on the same matrix reaches 0.1705, failing 341 of 2,000 decodes as 73 logical cosets and 268 non-convergences.
