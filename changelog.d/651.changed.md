The tempered Potts sweep records why it scales the accumulated local field
rather than its parts (#651). Dropping two whole-array temporaries per sweep is
available for free and was refused by #571 for not being bitwise; `CLAUDE.md`
now permits backing off bitwise to a declared tolerance, so the question was
re-opened and **measured**. The reassociation is not a rounding difference:
`h + sum J` cancels, so the cheaper form's absolute error is set by the
magnitudes of the parts while the result is near zero. Over 50,000 sites the
median is 1.00 unit of the last place and the maximum 201,145, with 2.9% past
the sweep's 16-unit guard — and those are the cancelling sites, whose
accumulated field is a median 7.4e-02 against 2.8e+00 for the rest. A near-zero
field is a near-uniform conditional, so the error concentrates on the sites
whose decision it is likeliest to flip. Refused, with the numbers held by a
test so the temptation does not return.

`kernels.factor_graph_log_density`'s left-to-right sum records that its bitwise
pin costs nothing (#651). Gathering the offsets and calling `np.sum` is slower
at every size from a thousand factors to a million — 0.60x to 0.77x — so the
ordered accumulate is also the fast form and the vectorization rule has nothing
to argue for here.

`PoissonEmission` and `BinomialEmission` fit their per-state mean with a matmul
(#651). The elementwise form builds an `(n_obs, n_states)` product before
reducing it — 80.7 MB at the fixture's scale — where `weights.T @ values` is
the same number with no intermediate: the expression is 8.55x faster (54.6 ms
to 6.4 ms) and the surrounding `reestimate` 2.27x (Poisson, 77.3 ms to 34.1 ms)
and 2.31x (Binomial, 79.1 ms to 34.3 ms). A matmul reduces in a different
order, so the fitted mean lands a relative 1.3e-14 from the elementwise fit:
three orders inside the 1e-11 this repository declares for a float64
comparison, which is the trade `CLAUDE.md` now permits. A test pins the
difference against a bound of 1e-3 of that tolerance, so a drift toward the
floor fails rather than hides. Closes #649.
