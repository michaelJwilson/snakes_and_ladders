`likelihood.pruning_torch` builds a leaf's indicator partial once per alignment
array rather than once per evaluation. The partial depends on the data and on
nothing being fitted, and 20 of a 20-taxon tree's 38 nodes are leaves, so a fit
rebuilt it thousands of times: a 20-taxon inference at 20 evaluations falls
from 19.52 s to 17.16 s (12.1%), with the post-order's own self time down 23%.

`search.infer`'s `lazy_top` is 6.29x faster at 20 taxa on a budget of 66
evaluations, reaching an identical log-likelihood and an identical topology
over two seeds, so a budgeted tree search should take it.

The post-order seeds its product with the first child's message rather than a
tensor of ones, and the rescale falls back to a scalar rather than a fresh
`ones_like` per node: 7.889 ms to 7.056 ms per forward-and-backward at 20 taxa
and 2,000 sites, 10.6%, with the value and every gradient entry bitwise what
they were.
