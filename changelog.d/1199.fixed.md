`sal.opt.hmm.forward_messages` and the Baum--Welch Python E step split their inputs once with `unbind` rather than slicing per step (#1199), so a gradient through the forward recursion no longer writes a zero block of the whole input per step. Forward plus backward takes 0.56 s over 200 segments of 100-3,000 positions (from 9.22 s), and 0.34 s at 50 x 1,000 positions over 16 states with each chain's own kernel per step (from 67.47 s). Values and gradients are unchanged bitwise.
