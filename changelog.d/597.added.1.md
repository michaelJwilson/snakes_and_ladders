`sal.learn.tabular`: Q-learning and SARSA over an enumerable
state set (issue #597). They share one loop and differ in the continuation term
of the update --- the greedy successor's value against the value of the action
the behaviour policy actually took --- which is the whole content of the module
and is why both are here: the cliff walk's claim that an on-policy and an
off-policy learner **disagree predictably** cannot be tested with one of them.

On `CliffWalk` Q-learning's greedy route hugs the cliff for the optimal **-7**
and SARSA's keeps a row clear for **-9**, while under the epsilon-greedy policy
they were learned with the order reverses: **-12.90** for SARSA against
**-25.54**, over 200 episodes each.
Only one of them should match value iteration, and that is asserted both ways ---
Q-learning's table is 2.14e-05 from `V*` on the 4x4 grid and SARSA's is 1.8889
from it, since its fixed point is `q_pi` for the policy it explores under.

Both are instrumentation rather than methods for a research problem, and the
measurement says why: 200 episodes cost 5.45 ms on the 4x4 grid against
`value_iteration`'s 322 us for the exact answer, so where the model is known
dynamic programming is ~170x cheaper than learning the same optimum.
