A per-observation covariate reaches the emission families from the caller
(#652). #631 gave the families an exposure and a trial count and refereed them
at the family; nothing above them passed one, so no fit could condition on one
and no end-to-end claim rested on them. Three seams now thread it.

`SpatioSequentialParams.covariate` is `(S, n_nodes)`, the observations' own
axes, defaulting to `None` — the model every construction before this field
described. `gated_log_density` passes it to every class's family,
`class_log_density` slices it by the same member block as the observations, and
the spatial M step selects the same members and moves the same axis, so a
family always scores and re-estimates each member against that member's own
exposure.

`baum_welch_family` takes a `(n_sequences, length)` covariate and reaches both
seams of the loop, the E step's scoring and the emission M step: a fit that
scores against an exposure and re-estimates without it is fitting two different
models. The HMM objectives store one beside their observations, for the same
reason the observations are there — it is fixed for the life of the objective,
where the family is rebuilt from `theta` on every call. A family that
conditions on nothing, `CategoricalEmission` and `PoissonEmission` among them,
refuses a covariate rather than ignoring it.

Refereed end to end, which is the point of the ticket: over an exposure
spanning a factor of 16, a negative-binomial Baum-Welch told the exposure
recovers a planted 2.0 and 9.0 as 1.97 and 8.81, and the same fit on the same
data not told it converges to 4.15 and 18.59 — the rate averaged over the
exposures, a factor of 2.1 high. A constant exposure of ones scores bitwise,
and through 200 EM iterations moves the fit a relative 5.1e-14 to 1.5e-13
across thread counts 1 to 8, two orders inside the 1e-11 declared for a float64
comparison.
