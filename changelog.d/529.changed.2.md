Each of the textbook's eleven problem statements now carries a discussion of
the algorithms it assumes, which of them is worth a budget at that problem's
own supported sizes, what each method requires of an instance before it may
run at all, and the application-specific property its validation rests on. A
shared subsection states the three axes every such comparison uses --- the
precondition, the cost in the unit the problem counts, and the size past
which no exact referee reaches --- and tabulates the assumption behind each
problem's referees beside what stops being refereed without it.
