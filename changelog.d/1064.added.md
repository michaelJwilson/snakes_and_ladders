The Rust coupled E step takes a continuous covariate (issue #1064). The negative binomial's exposure is factored out of the table: the total's table is `B = NegativeBinomialEmission.count_log_factor + r ln r + y ln mu` by count, and the kernel adds `y ln c - (y + r) ln t` per observation, one logarithm per score: 262.9 ulp from the family's `log_density` (512 declared), chosen over the family's order (2.3 ulp) for a stress E step of 1.4-1.6 s against 2.4 s in the kernel. At the ci instance of the new `spatio_sequential_counts_covariate` fixture (64,000 log-normal exposures) one E step is 0.145 s at +5 MiB, against 58.5 s and a 9.3 GB peak for the table by count and distinct exposure; at its stress instance (5,041 vertices, 2,000 positions), one E step is 5.3 s against the NumPy oracle's 28.7 s and one field 13.1 s against 364.8 s, where that table would be 2.9e13 bytes. `covariate_tolerance` codes a tabulated covariate on a power-of-two grid within a stated per-observation bound (`covariate_grid_error`); the integer trial count is exact on it at scale 1, and skipping `np.unique` takes the stress E step from 7.5 s to 5.3 s. `None` keeps the distinct-value table bitwise. `fit_spatio_sequential` and the three `likelihood.spatio_sequential` entries thread it; the NumPy backend refuses it.
