The codes whose maximum-likelihood decoder is closed form
(`snakes_and_ladders.sim.elementary_codes`, issue #594): repetition, single
parity check, Hamming and extended Hamming, Golay and extended Golay, and the
CRC #593 needs. Each brings a referee that does not run out where enumeration
does: the single parity check's exact posterior **is** the tanh rule, pinning
the check-node update every LDPC and turbo decode here performs to 4.4e-15
against brute force; repetition carries an analytic error probability on the
erasure and binary symmetric channels, which no fixture had; and Hamming and
Golay are perfect, so the sphere-packing count is asserted as an **equality**
over integers rather than a bound. Reed--Solomon over `GF(2^m)` follows
(`snakes_and_ladders.sim.galois`, `snakes_and_ladders.sim.reed_solomon`): the
first code here that is MDS, so `d = n - k + 1` is asserted as an equality --
5 over all 512 codewords of RS(7,3) -- rather than a Singleton bound, and the
first algebraic decoder, Berlekamp--Massey with Chien search and Forney, exact
on 900 of 900 draws at up to `t` errors. Past `t` it is bounded-distance and
says so: over 300 draws beyond the radius it refused 260 and returned a wrong
codeword 40 times, recovering none by luck.
