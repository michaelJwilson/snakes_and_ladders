Canonical control problems, so a learner is pinned against a known optimum
before it is pointed at a research problem (`sal.learn.canonical`,
issue #597). Four of them, each exposing something the others do not: a chain
whose greedy optimum is the near reward and whose true optimum is at the far
end, a gridworld for credit assignment over a horizon, a cliff walk whose
shortest route runs along a row of penalties, and Towers of Hanoi, whose
optimum is the integer `2^d - 1`.

`value_iteration` is the oracle and is a **second computation**, not a second
opinion: it sweeps the Bellman operator over an enumerated reachable set where
`learn.exact.exact_optimal_value` recurses over trajectories, and the suite
holds them to each other at 1e-12 on every fixture. Value iteration refuses
rather than returning an unbounded optimum where a cycle pays.

Both learners measured reach the optimum from every start of the 4x4 grid:
REINFORCE at 300 iterations of 16 episodes and PPO at 60 of 16 --- 5x fewer
iterations at 5.9x the cost each, so 3.1 s against 3.6 s of wall clock, which
is the two-sided statement a ratio alone would hide.

**Two fixtures the plan listed are not here, and that is reported rather than
approximated.** `Environment.step` is deterministic by contract and
`learn.exact`'s enumeration depends on it, so the `k`-armed bandit (whose
tension is a stochastic reward) and the slippery Frozen Lake cannot be written
against this protocol without widening it for every implementer and
invalidating that oracle. They are on `TICKETS.md`; a deterministic stand-in
would look like the problem and test nothing about it.
