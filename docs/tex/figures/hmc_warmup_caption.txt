What a warm-up buys, on the target whose answer is closed form. Two chains of 1200 draws at 12 leapfrog steps run from the same start on a 2-dimensional Gaussian of mean (20.0, -2.0) and covariance ((100.0, 2.5), (2.5, 0.25)), whose eigenvalues differ by a factor of 534. One adapts its step size and mass diagonal over 400 discarded proposals and then runs at fixed values; the other keeps the starting step of 0.6 and unit mass throughout. Upper: the running mean of the wider coordinate, the one a step small enough to be stable on the narrower direction crosses by a random walk, against its exact value of 20; the start sits two of its standard deviations away. Lower: the same error in units of that coordinate's exact standard deviation of 10. The running mean reaches the final 0.1 standard deviations and stays there at draw 211 with the warm-up and draw 323 without; at the last draw the error is 0.000 and 0.019. The warm-up settled on a step of 1.273 and a mass diagonal of (0.014, 3.912), accepting 0.63 over the warm-up against a target of 0.65. Both chains are correct: the acceptance is 0.65 adapted and 0.90 fixed, and each is a kernel with the right stationary distribution, so what separates them is cost per effective draw --- 2.08e-02 effective draws per gradient with the warm-up, its own gradients charged to it, against 5.85e-03 without.
