## The constraint this audit is read under

#813 is referential: it changes how a problem is called and never what a call
returns or what referees it. An oracle is a rung of the ladder and a rung is
never merged with another rung, so where two functions are two rungs --- the two
expectation--maximization loops, the NumPy and Rust pruning --- the audit
records the inconsistency and moves nothing. A fold lands here only where the
new path returns the old bits and a test says so.

What the table says, in the order it costs a caller:

1. **The seed is passed three ways.** Inside `params` (HMM, count pairs,
   Potts chain), as an `rng` beside `params` (mixture, coupled), as both
   (`simulate_alignment` takes `rng`; `SimulationParams` carries `seed`).
   `sim/CLAUDE.md`'s rule is "a generator, never a seed"; two problems and the
   Potts chain keep the exception `count_pairs` states for itself.
2. **The argument order for "evaluate" is `(model, data)` on the tree and the
   coupled model and `(data, model)` on the mixture and the HMM.**
3. **`backend` names three different things**: the kernel (`Backend`, 44
   `backend: Backend` parameters), the parallel map (`infer(..., backend: str = 'serial')`), and a
   triple of callables (`CoupledBackend`, one problem). The second and third
   are the same word for a different seam.
4. **A schedule is spelled twice**: `TempSchedule` (four annealers, the HMC
   initializers) and `Sequence[float]` of betas or temperatures (seven
   functions in `sample.annealed`, `sample.tempered`, `sample.potts_mcmc`,
   `sample.hmc`). A caller who built a `TempSchedule` cannot hand it to
   `parallel_tempering`.
5. **`iterations` and `max_iterations` are both the same bound**
   (`decode_turbo` against `decode` and `decode_syndrome`).
6. **Sixteen `load_*_params` functions have one signature and sixteen bodies.**
   Root `fixtures.py` already holds the preamble they shared (#132); what is
   left in each is the per-field validation, which is where they differ.

## Seams: what exists, what one-way calling needs, what is refused

The 16 declared seams are in the 2026-09-19 review. What they span, per intent:
**fit** and **sample** on a tensor are one shape through `Objective` (16
public implementers), **emit** is one through `EmissionFamily`, and **bound** through
`Surrogate`. Nothing spans **simulate**, **evaluate on a discrete state**,
**decode**, **load**, or **anneal**.

Proposed and decided by the three-consumer rule and the constraint:

- **`Simulator` protocol, `(params, rng) -> Dataset`**: 9 simulators would
  implement it, and `qa` and the notebooks call each by name today. **Earns
  it** on implementers alone, and is the fold that fixes finding 1. Not here: it
  moves the seed from `params` to `rng` on three problems, whose fixture draws
  are then re-refereed at every tier.
- **One `Schedule`**: `TempSchedule` gains a constructor from a sequence, the
  seven `betas`/`temperatures` signatures take it. **Earns it** (7 consumers).
  Bitwise, since a `TempSchedule` over the same floats returns the same
  floats; deferred only because it touches seven signatures across four
  modules.
- **`Decoder` protocol, `(code, llr, *, max_iterations) -> Decoding`**: 3
  implementers, 2 consumers (`qa`, the codes notebook). **Under the rule**;
  the rename `iterations -> max_iterations` stands alone.
- **`CoupledBackend -> Backend`**: one problem, one consumer. Under the rule
  as a seam, but it is a *second spelling* of one that exists, which the rule
  does not admit either way; fold, then seam `spatio_sequential`.
- **One `load_params`**: 16 identical signatures. What differs is the
  validation; a `Params` protocol with `from_declared` classmethods would
  move sixteen bodies and change no bits. Earns it; deferred as a move of
  16 modules.
